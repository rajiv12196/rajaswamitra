# Mutation of land records - general procedure (sample; replace with your State Land Revenue Code and rules)

Mutation (dakhil-kharij / pherphar / namantaran) records a change in ownership or possession in the record of rights after sale, gift, inheritance, partition, court decree or acquisition. It is a fiscal entry for revenue purposes and does not by itself confer title.

Typical steps:
1. Application or intimation received (from the registering office, the heir, or the party), with supporting documents such as the registered deed, death certificate, legal heir / succession certificate, or decree.
2. Entry in the mutation register by the village revenue official (Patwari / Talathi / VAO).
3. Public notice and individual notice to all interested persons, inviting objections within the period fixed by the State rules.
4. If there is no objection, the competent revenue officer attests the mutation. If objections are received, the case is treated as disputed and decided after hearing the parties, by a reasoned order.
5. The record of rights (jamabandi / khatauni / 7-12 extract) is updated and a copy is given to the parties.
6. Appeal or revision lies to the higher revenue authority as provided in the State Code.

Principles of natural justice apply: notice to affected persons, opportunity of hearing, and a speaking order.
